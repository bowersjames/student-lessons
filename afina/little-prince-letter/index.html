How to write a great End

People remember the End of a story best. A great End answers 3 questions.

  • Is the problem finished?
  • How did I feel, and why?
  • What changed?

Which End is better?

Sam wrote a story about a night when his train broke down. At the station, he met a boy with golden hair who laughed and pointed at a star. Here are 2 Ends for Sam's story. Tap the better one.

The pilot goes back

The book never tells us what happened next. Here is a Beginning and a Middle. You write the End.

Beginning

One year after the little prince left, I flew back to the desert. I wanted to know if he was happy, and if his rose was safe.

Middle

I sat under his star and waited. The night was cold and very quiet. Then, just before morning, I heard a small laugh behind me.

Your End goes here.

Write 2 different Ends

On paper, by hand. You are the pilot, so write "I".

  • Write End 1 in 3 or 4 sentences, in the past.
  • Write End 2. Make it really different. Maybe the laugh is the little prince, and maybe it is someone else.
  • Next to each End, draw beside the sentences that answer each question.
Stuck?

When I turned around, I saw ______.
I felt ______ because ______.
Now, when I ______, I ______.

Feeling words you know: nervous, terrified, relieved, proud, calm, sad.
