How to write a great End

1

Remember the lesson

Remember the 3 parts of a story and the feeling words.

Beginning, Middle and End

About 2,300 years ago, the Greek thinker Aristotle said a whole story has a beginning, a middle and an end. Film writers still use his idea. They call it the three-act structure.

Why the End matters most

The End is the last thing your reader reads, so it is the part they remember.

A weak End can spoil a good story. A great End answers 3 questions.

  • Is the problem finished?
  • How did I feel?
  • What changed?

You will draw these symbols on your paper.

Study a real End

See which question the End of The Little Prince does not answer.

The last page

On the last page, the pilot draws the desert and one star. It is the place where the little prince came to Earth, and where he left.

The pilot believes the little prince got home to his planet. But he still worries. Did the sheep eat the rose? Is the little prince happy?

So he asks you, the reader, for help. If you are ever in the desert in Africa, wait under the star. If a boy with golden hair laughs and won't answer your questions, write to the pilot quickly. Tell him the little prince came back.

Which question is still open?

Look at the 3 questions. Which question does the last page not answer? Tap it.

Your task

Write a short story with a strong End. Write it as a letter to the pilot. Imagine you met the little prince, and tell the pilot what happened. Your End can finish his story.

  • In the Beginning, tell him where you were, when it was, and what the problem was.
  • In the Middle, tell him what happened when you met the little prince.
  • In the End, finish your problem, tell him the little prince got home, and say how you felt and what changed for you.

In the book, the pilot meets him in the desert in Africa. In your letter, you can choose another place, like the beach, school, or the sky during your skydive.

Weak End, strong End

Compare two Ends for another reader's letter.

Sam's letter

Another reader wrote to the pilot. Here are the Beginning and the Middle.

Dear Pilot,

Beginning

Last summer, I was on a train to the mountains with my family. At night, the train broke down, and we waited for hours at a tiny station.

Middle

When I walked outside, I saw a boy with golden hair. He was sitting alone and looking at the sky. I asked him where he came from. He didn't answer. He only laughed and pointed at a star.

Find the "When" sentence. Where is the comma?

After "outside". The comma ends the "When" part. Then comes what happened.

4

Write your own End

Plan, write and check your letter by hand. Take the most care with the End.

Plan on a story mountain

Draw a mountain like this on your paper. Write a few words at each place, not full sentences.

  • At the foot, write where and when it was, and the problem.
  • At the top, write the biggest moment, when you met the little prince.
  • On the way down, write a few words next to each symbol. The End gets the most space.

Write it by hand

By hand, on paper. Start with "Dear Pilot,".

  • Write 1 or 2 sentences for the Beginning.
  • Write 2 sentences for the Middle. Start with a "When" sentence.
  • Write 3 or 4 sentences for the End. Each symbol on your mountain needs at least one. Take most care with this part.
  • Then sign your name.
