Let's write better Ends

Readers remember the End most, because it's the last thing they read.

Think of a film you loved until the last 5 minutes. A weak End can change how we feel about the whole story. A great End answers 3 questions.

  1. 1Is the problem finished?
  2. 2How did I feel, and why?
  3. 3What changed?

Here are 3 short stories with weak Ends. Let's find what's missing in each one, and then you can write a better End.

Story 1

On Saturday, I lost my phone at South Bank. I looked everywhere, and I started to panic. Then a woman tapped me on the shoulder.

End

I felt relieved because she was so kind, and I smiled all the way home. Now, I always check my bag twice.

What do you think is missing?

Story 2

One year after the little prince left, I flew back to the desert. I sat under his star and waited all night. Just before morning, I heard a small laugh behind me.

End

When I turned around, the little prince was there. He told me his rose was safe. Now, I fly back to the desert every year.

What do you think is missing?

Your turn

Now try writing a new End on paper, as the pilot. Try one sentence for each question.

Some words that might help

  • shocked
  • excited
  • so happy that I cried
  • not alone any more
  • because I thought I'd never see him again

Story 3

Last term, I had to give a speech to my whole class. My hands were shaking, and I forgot my first line. Then my friend smiled at me from the front row.

End

I remembered the words and finished my speech. Everyone clapped. I felt proud because I didn't give up.

What do you think is missing?

Your turn

Now try writing a new End on paper. Try one sentence for each question.

Some words that might help

  • brave
  • confident
  • proud
  • not so scared
  • Now, when I speak in class, ...
